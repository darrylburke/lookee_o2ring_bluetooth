Oxygen drops by hour of night

Drops of 3 % or more. “With pulse surge” means the drop was followed by a pulse rise of ≥6 bpm – a sign it was a real event that disturbed sleep rather than measurement noise.

Repeating drop cycles

Stretches where oxygen rises and falls in a regular 30–70 second rhythm – the signature of breathing events repeating back to back, rather than isolated dips. A pattern indicator only: a ring cannot tell what kind of event it is.

Estimated sleep window

Estimated from movement alone, so treat it as a rough guide: a still, awake person looks asleep, the ring's motion scale is uncalibrated, and this kind of estimate is known to overstate sleep. Best used to compare your own nights.

Please note
Everything on this page is calculated from the readings of a consumer finger oximeter, which is accurate to about ±2–3 %. The drop counts and ODI figures are informational, meant for following your own nights over time.

None of it is a diagnosis, and a quiet-looking night does not rule anything out. If your sleep, breathing or daytime tiredness worries you, take it up with a doctor.
